## Phase 1 Output: 5 Review Reports

### EIC Review (Prof. Santos) — Summary Only

**Summary**: This paper investigates how Taiwan's declining birth rate affects management strategies at private universities. Using a mixed-methods approach combining survey data from 42 private universities with semi-structured interviews of 15 university presidents, the paper identifies four strategic response categories: enrollment diversification, international recruitment, program restructuring, and campus consolidation. The contribution lies in providing empirical evidence for strategic adaptation patterns in a context undergoing severe demographic contraction.

**Strengths**: Timely and policy-relevant topic; good access to university presidents; clear categorization of strategies.

**Weaknesses**: Limited sample representativeness (42 of ~100+ private universities); strategy categories may be superficial without outcome data; no longitudinal evidence.

**Overall**: 4 (Borderline Accept) — Important topic with useful data, but evaluation lacks outcome measures.

---

### R1 Methodology Review (Prof. Tanaka) — Key Points

**Strengths**: Mixed methods design is appropriate; interview protocol well-described; survey instrument validated.

**Weaknesses**:
- W1: Survey response rate of 42% is acceptable but not strong; no non-response bias analysis
- W2: Interview sample selection criteria unclear — were the 15 presidents purposively sampled? How?
- W3: No statistical tests for survey results — only descriptive statistics reported
- W4: Mixed methods integration is weak — qual and quant analyzed separately without triangulation

**Overall**: 3 (Borderline Reject) — Methodology has significant gaps in statistical rigor and integration.

---

### R2 Domain Review (Prof. Chen) — Key Points

**Strengths**: Excellent understanding of Taiwan's higher education landscape; good use of MOE enrollment data.

**Weaknesses**:
- W1: Missing key references: Chang & Ho (2023) on university closure patterns; Wu (2022) on enrollment prediction models
- W2: Strategy categorization overlaps with Lin (2021) framework without adequate differentiation
- W3: No discussion of government policy responses (MOE consolidation incentives, exit mechanisms)

**Overall**: 4 (Borderline Accept) — Strong domain grounding but literature gaps weaken the novelty claim.

---

### R3 Perspective Review (Prof. Johnson) — Key Points

**Strengths**: Connects to broader organizational survival literature; practical implications for policymakers.

**Weaknesses**:
- W1: No connection to organizational decline theory (Weitzel & Jonsson, 1989) which directly applies
- W2: Missing stakeholder perspective — student and faculty impacts of these strategies unexamined
- W3: No international comparison (Japan, Korea face similar demographic challenges)

**Overall**: 4 (Borderline Accept) — Could be much stronger with cross-disciplinary framing.

---

### Devil's Advocate Report (Prof. Weber) — Key Points

**Strongest Counter-Argument**: The paper claims to identify "management strategies" but actually documents self-reported intentions. Without outcome data (enrollment changes, financial performance, survival rates), we cannot distinguish effective strategies from wishful thinking. A university president saying "we plan to recruit internationally" is not evidence of a strategy working — it may be a strategy that accelerates failure if poorly implemented.

**CRITICAL Issue**: DA-C1 — No outcome validation. The paper cannot distinguish effective from ineffective strategies without longitudinal outcome data.

**MAJOR Issues**: DA-M1 — Survivorship bias (only surveyed existing universities, not those that already closed); DA-M2 — No cost-benefit analysis of strategies.

---

## Phase 2 Output: Editorial Decision

**Decision: 3 — Borderline Reject**

The paper addresses an important and timely topic with good institutional access. However, the Devil's Advocate raises a CRITICAL concern: without outcome data, the paper documents intentions rather than effective strategies. Combined with methodology gaps (no statistical testing, weak mixed-methods integration) and missing literature, the paper needs significant revision.

### Revision Roadmap (Top 3 Priority 1 Items)

| # | Issue | Source | Fix | Effort |
|---|-------|--------|-----|--------|
| R1 | Add outcome measures (enrollment trends, financial data) for each strategy | DA-C1, R1 | Link MOE enrollment data to survey respondents | High |
| R2 | Add statistical analysis (chi-square, regression) to survey data | R1-W3 | Reanalyze existing data with appropriate tests | Medium |
| R3 | Strengthen mixed methods integration with triangulation | R1-W4 | Redesign analysis to cross-validate qual and quant findings | Medium |
